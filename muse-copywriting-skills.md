# MUSE Copywriting Skill

## 1. Brand Identity & Core Positioning
- **Brand Name:** Use `MUSE` in headlines, eyebrows, and navigation; use `Muse` in body copy and inline UI labels.
- **Category:** Colour intelligence for fashion (web-first platform).
- **Core Promise:** *"Before the first stitch."*
- **What MUSE Does:** Profiles skin tone, undertone, and contrast into a five-tone palette; reads garment colour from fabric photos; scores colour harmony with swap suggestions; previews combinations on an interactive full-body mannequin across 8 looks, 6 light grades, 360° silhouette turns, and 5 recolourable tops; saves matches for repeatable client workflows.
- **What MUSE Is NOT:** Never position MUSE as generative-AI virtual try-on, an AR fitting room, a generic "what season are you?" quiz, a thrift/resale marketplace, or a native mobile app.
- **Differentiator:** Deterministic, explainable colour science paired with a body-level mannequin preview—and **zero selfie or face-image uploads** in v1.

---

## 2. Target Audience & Mindset
Write for three primary personas and one secondary persona:
1. **Independent Fashion Designers & Small Ateliers:** Need repeatable colour decisions, fewer wasted fabric samples, and objective language for fittings.
2. **Stylists & Image / Colour Consultants:** Manage multiple clients, need rapid palette reads, and must defend recommendations with authority.
3. **Boutique Studio Owners & Small Fashion Teams:** Want a shared client colour workflow without heavy enterprise PLM software.
4. **Fashion-Conscious Individuals (Secondary):** Want a personal five-tone palette and a second opinion before buying or commissioning a garment.

**Target Mindset to Address:**
- *"I know what I like, but I need to explain why it works."*
- *"I keep testing the same colour combinations from scratch."*
- *"I do not want another black-box AI tool or selfie upload."*
- *"I want to see the colour on a body before I cut fabric or commit."*

---

## 3. Tone, Voice & Language Rules
- **Tone in One Line:** Quietly luxurious, exacting, and warm—like an experienced studio director, never a SaaS growth marketer.
- **Spelling & Locale:** Strictly **British English** (`colour`, `fitting`, `atelier`, `personalised`, `recolourable`, `organise`, `standardise`).
- **Sentence Rhythm:** Keep sentences short, crisp, and concrete. Let photography, swatches, and the mannequin carry the emotion.
- **Lead with the Outcome:** Sell the decision made in the fitting room, not the underlying software mechanics.
- **Confident, Never Absolute:** Colour guidance should feel expert and grounded in light and undertone logic, never dictatorial about personal taste.

### Vocabulary — Use These
- `colour intelligence`, `the rail, in colour`, `before the first stitch`
- `a read you can explain`, `on the body`, `studio light`, `save the match`
- `client-ready`, `repeatable`, `quieter, clearer, sharper`
- Studio nouns & verbs: `palette`, `rail`, `seam`, `fabric`, `sample`, `fitting`, `drape`, `undertone`, `contrast`, `yardage`, `swatch`

### Vocabulary — Strictly Avoid
- **Hype & SaaS Clichés:** `AI-powered`, `AI magic`, `revolutionary`, `game-changing`, `unlock`, `elevate`, `transform`, `seamless`, `effortless`, `10x`, `the future of fashion`
- **Generic Wellness/Quiz Tropes:** `find your perfect style`, `your personal colour journey`, `discover your season`
- **Unverified Claims:** Never invent user counts, brand logos, accuracy percentages, or fake testimonials. Rely on product mechanics and demonstration.

## 4. Objection Handling Matrix
Every landing page pass must quietly resolve these five user doubts:
1. **"Is this just another colour quiz?"** -> Emphasise that MUSE checks real garment photos and previews combinations on a full-body mannequin.
2. **"Is this accurate?"** -> Explain the three deterministic inputs (skin tone, undertone, contrast) and show the harmony logic rather than claiming magic.
3. **"Do I have to upload my face?"** -> State clearly that profiles are self-declared and checked with **no face photo or selfie upload**.
4. **"Why pay for this?"** -> Tie paid tiers to repeatable multi-client workflows, six studio lighting grades, and saved fitting history.
5. **"Can I use this with clients?"** -> Highlight shared palettes, client profiles, and workspace seats in Studio and Atelier tiers.

## 5. Landing Page Section Blueprint

### Global Navigation
- Keep links clean and studio-focused: `Explore` / `Colour` / `Studio` / `Motion` / `Process` / `Pricing`
- Primary CTA must match the active modal destination (`Build a palette` or `Get started`).

### 1. Hero (`#explore`)
- **Job:** State the concrete outcome immediately and invite a free profile.
- **Structure:** Eyebrow (`Colour Intelligence`) + Concrete Outcome Headline (*"See the palette on the body, before the first stitch."*) + 1–2 sentence subhead explaining profile, garment check, and mannequin preview + Primary CTA + Trust microcopy (*"No selfie upload required. Web-first studio."*).

### 2. Colour / Atelier Band (`#atelier`)
- **Job:** Translate the three core capabilities into a problem-to-outcome sequence.
- **Structure:** Headline (*"The rail, in colour."*) + 3 numbered moves (`01 Profile the person`, `02 Read the garment`, `03 Preview on the body`) + secondary CTA (`Explore the rail` / `Read the atelier notes`).

### 3. Virtual Styling Studio (`#mannequin`)
- **Job:** Make the interactive mannequin feel like a working studio tool, not decoration.
- **Structure:** Headline (*"On the body."*) + explanation of 8 outfits and 6 light grades + **1-line action prompt** telling the visitor what to click first.

### 4. 360° Motion Study (`#spin-showcase`)
- **Job:** Connect silhouette rotation to real garment decisions (drape, fabric weight, colour across a seam).
- **Structure:** Label (`360° study / 01`) + Headline (*"The form, in the round."*) + 2-sentence studio rationale + brief drag/view microcopy.

### 5. Editorial Motion (`#motion`)
- **Job:** Build visual desire and prove how light shifts tone on cloth.
- **Structure:** Headline (*"The edit, in motion."*) + restraint-led copy on studio light + clean tile labels (`Her edit / Crimson`, `His edit / Wool Suit`) + playback hint (`Muted loops. Pause to hold the frame.`).

### 6. How Muse Works (`#features`)
- **Job:** Remove workflow uncertainty from start to finish.
- **Structure:** Headline (*"Five moves from skin read to saved match."*) + 5 outcome-named steps (`01 Skin read`, `02 Styling studio`, `03 Motion study`, `04 Colour try-on`, `05 Saved matches`) + CTA (`Start with a skin read`).

### 7. Pricing (`#pricing`)
- **Job:** Help solo users, stylists, and studio teams self-select without friction.
- **Structure:** State **who each tier is for** before listing features:
  - **Read (Free):** Individuals testing a personal palette or checking a single garment (`Start free`).
  - **Studio ($19/mo or $15/mo annual):** Independent designers, stylists, and consultants managing multiple clients (`Choose Studio`).
  - **Atelier ($49/mo or $39/mo annual):** Boutiques and fashion teams standardising colour across a shared rail (`Talk to the studio`).
- Always clarify billing terms (`Monthly plans can be cancelled anytime. Annual billing saves 20%.`).

### 8. Studio Close (`#studio`) & Footer
- **Job:** Capture ready visitors across individual and studio paths, plus establish trust.
- **Structure:** Headline (*"Build the look before the garment."*) + dual CTAs (`Start a free palette` / `Talk to the studio`) + complete legal footer (`Privacy`, `Terms`, `Contact`, `© 2026 MUSE`).

### 9. Modals (CTA Alignment Rule)
Never send a user to a mismatched modal. Align modal copy strictly with the clicked button:
- **Personal / Free Modal:** *"Create your colour profile."* -> `Build my palette`
- **Studio Access Modal:** *"Open your Muse Studio."* -> `Continue to Studio`
- **Atelier / Early Access Modal:** *"Bring MUSE to your atelier."* -> `Request studio access`
