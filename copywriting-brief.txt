1. APP NAME & CORE FUNCTION
---------------------------

App name
MUSE

Core function
MUSE is a web-first colour-intelligence platform for fashion. It helps a designer,
stylist, or client answer one practical question: "Which colours and combinations
suit this person, this garment, and this outfit?"

What it actually does
- Profiles skin tone, undertone, and contrast.
- Builds a personal five-tone palette with a clear explanation.
- Reads the colour of a garment from a fabric photo.
- Scores and explains colour harmony, including suggested swaps.
- Previews the palette on an interactive full-body mannequin.
- Shows eight complete looks and six light grades.
- Includes a 360-degree silhouette study.
- Includes five try-on tops that can be recoloured by mask.
- Saves colour matches and client looks for later comparison.
- Gives designers a repeatable client workflow rather than one-off guesswork.

What it is not
- Not a generative-AI virtual try-on product.
- Not an AR fitting room.
- Not a generic "what season are you?" quiz.
- Not a thrift, resale, or tailor marketplace.
- Not a native mobile app yet. The current experience is web-first.

Core promise
"Before the first stitch."

Supporting idea
MUSE makes colour decisions visible, explainable, and repeatable before time or money
is spent on a sample.

Why this matters for copy
Lead with the decision and the outcome, not the technology. "Colour intelligence"
is the product category; "AI" is not the selling point. The differentiator is
deterministic, explainable colour science plus a mannequin preview.


2. TARGET AUDIENCE
------------------

Primary audience
1. Independent fashion designers and small atelier teams.
   They need repeatable colour decisions, fewer wasted samples, and language they
   can use with clients.

2. Stylists and image / colour consultants.
   They manage multiple clients, need fast palette reads, and need to explain a
   recommendation with confidence.

3. Boutique studio owners and small fashion teams.
   They want a shared client workflow without enterprise software.

Secondary audience
4. Fashion-conscious individuals.
   They want a personal palette, a second opinion on a garment, and confidence
   before buying or commissioning.

Best-fit user mindset
- "I know what I like, but I need to explain why it works."
- "I keep testing the same colour combinations from scratch."
- "I do not want another black-box AI tool or another selfie upload."
- "I want to see the colour on a body before I commit."

Not the current core audience
- Gen Z streetwear shoppers looking for resale or outfit inspiration.
- Consumers looking for AR try-on or body measurement.
- Large enterprise fashion brands needing a full PLM integration.

Primary user jobs
- Build a reliable palette for a client.
- Check whether a fabric or garment works.
- Show the result on a body.
- Save the match and return to it later.
- Explain the decision in a fitting room or client presentation.


3. KEY PAIN POINT & VALUE PROPOSITION
-------------------------------------

The pain
Colour decisions are slow, subjective, and repeated constantly. Designers rely on
experience and trial; clients rely on guesswork and social media. A poor colour
choice is often discovered late, after fabric, time, or a sample has already been
committed.

The consequences
- Re-sampling and wasted material.
- Second-guessing in fittings.
- Inconsistent advice across clients.
- Recommendations that are hard to defend.
- Palettes that look good online but behave differently in daylight, evening light,
  or on camera.

Current alternatives and their gaps
- Colour-analysis quizzes: give a palette but do not show it on a body or check a
  garment.
- AI try-on tools: often require selfies, feel like a black box, and carry privacy
  and trust concerns.
- Designer intuition / spreadsheets: flexible, but hard to repeat and explain.
- Generic wardrobe apps: organise clothes but do not support a designer's colour
  workflow.

Value proposition
MUSE turns skin tone, undertone, contrast, and garment colour into a clear palette
and a body-level preview you can explain, repeat, and save — before a sample is cut.

Why it is better
- Explainable: the result is a colour read, not a magic verdict.
- Repeatable: the same profile and rules can be used again.
- Visual: the palette is shown on a mannequin, not only as swatches.
- Private by design: no selfie or face-image processing in v1.
- Fast: the target is a usable palette in under 60 seconds and a garment check in
  under 3 minutes. Treat these as product targets until they are tested in public copy.
- Low-cost at the point of use: the browser-rendered mannequin avoids a per-render
  generative-AI cost.

Main objections the copy must answer
- "Is this just another colour quiz?"
  Answer: it checks a garment and shows the result on a body.
- "Is this accurate?"
  Answer: explain the inputs and the colour logic; show the result; avoid absolute
  claims about taste.
- "Do I have to upload my face?"
  Answer: no. The profile is self-declared and checked, with no face image in v1.
- "Why would I pay for this?"
  Answer: repeatable client work, saved matches, and a faster path to a decision.
- "Can I use this with clients?"
  Answer: Studio and Atelier tiers support saved looks, shared palettes, and
  multi-client work.

Proof needed before final copy
- At least two designer or stylist testimonials.
- One concrete before/after workflow example.
- A named colour consultant or industry co-sign, if available.
- A clear demo of the mannequin and garment check.
Without proof, the page must rely on the product demonstration itself. Do not invent
usage numbers, client names, or accuracy claims.


4. BRAND TONE & AESTHETIC
-------------------------

Tone in one line
Quietly luxurious, exacting, and warm — like a studio director, not a growth marketer.

Voice principles
- Lead with the decision, not the technology.
- Use studio language: palette, rail, seam, fabric, sample, fitting, light, match.
- Be specific. Replace "effortless style" with "a colour read you can explain."
- Confident, never absolute. Colour advice should feel expert, not authoritarian.
- Short sentences. Let the product and photography carry the emotion.
- Use British English: colour, fitting, atelier, personalised.

Words and phrases that fit
- colour intelligence
- the rail, in colour
- before the first stitch
- a read you can explain
- on the body
- studio light
- save the match
- client-ready
- repeatable
- quieter, clearer, sharper

Words and phrases to avoid
- AI-powered, AI magic, revolutionary, game-changing
- unlock, elevate, transform, seamless, effortless
- "find your perfect style"
- "your personal colour journey"
- "10x your styling"
- "the future of fashion"
- generic fashion-tech hype with no product proof

Visual and verbal identity
- Editorial fashion, not SaaS.
- Warm neutrals: shell, rose, ember, brick, ink.
- Deep near-black studio backgrounds.
- Oxblood / brick accent for emphasis.
- Montserrat display type with Archivo body copy.
- Photography should feel like a studio study: fabric, light, drape, silhouette.
- Motion should be restrained: muted loops, slow turns, no jump-cut hype.

Brand naming note
Use "MUSE" for the brand in headlines and "Muse" only where the existing UI uses
title case. Standardise one form before launch.

6. RECOMMENDED MESSAGING ORDER
------------------------------

1. Hero: the outcome — a colour decision you can see and explain.
2. Colour: the three moves — profile, garment check, mannequin.
3. Mannequin: the product proof — try it on the body.
4. 360: the form proof — read colour and drape together.
5. Motion: the emotional proof — the palette in studio light.
6. Features: the full workflow — from skin read to saved match.
7. Pricing: the right tier for the way you work.
8. Studio: the final step — start a palette or talk to the studio.

Message pillars
- Read the person, not the trend.
- Check the garment before the sample.
- See it on the body.
- Keep the match.
- Explain the decision.

Recommended hero direction for the next copy pass
Keep "Colour Vision." only if the rest of the hero makes the outcome literal. A
stronger conversion hero would pair the editorial line with one concrete promise,
for example: "See the palette on the body before the first stitch." Use the final
version only after the product demo and CTA path are confirmed.


7. OPEN ITEMS TO CONFIRM BEFORE FINAL COPY
------------------------------------------

1. Is the primary conversion a free account, an early-access request, or a studio
   demo? The current modals mix all three.
2. Is pricing live, or is it still a concept? Do not promise checkout if billing is
   not connected.
3. What can be claimed publicly today: no face uploads, deterministic colour logic,
   saved looks, client profiles, and the mannequin demo? Confirm each.
4. Do we have at least two real testimonials or a named consultant co-sign? If not,
   keep proof product-led and avoid invented authority.
5. Is the launch market UK, EU, or US English? The current page uses British spelling.
6. Does "MUSE" or "Muse" win as the standard written brand form?
7. Should the page sell to designers first, or to individual clients first? The
   current page speaks to both and risks diluting the primary CTA.
8. Is the 360 study and the five-top try-on available to free users, or only as a
   demo? Align pricing copy with actual access.
